What we do

Computers still listen to Nepali badly, and speak it worse.

Most speech software is built for a few big languages and then stretched over the rest. Nepali gets the stretch: recognisers tested on someone reading a sentence aloud, and voices that sound like a word list.

We build for the Nepali people actually speak. That means bad phone lines, English dropped into the middle of a sentence, and spelling nobody quite agreed on.

We measure before we say anything. Every result we publish comes with the test sets, the scorer and the other systems we ran, including the ones that beat us.

And we keep our models small enough to run on your own machine, so your recordings and your text do not have to leave it.

The name

फिस्टेfiste

फिस्टो, or फिस्टे, is what we call the small warblers you hear in the hills before you see them. The logo is one of those birds, looking out over the mountains.

The models are named for sound too. श्रव्य (shravya) means audible, what can be heard. सुसेली (suseli) is the tune you whistle to yourself, and one warbler is even named for it: the सुसेली फिस्टो, the Whistler's warbler.
