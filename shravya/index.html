What it does

Most Nepali speech recognition is tested on someone reading a sentence aloud. People do not talk like that.

They talk over a bad phone line, drop English words into a Nepali sentence, start again halfway, and spell nothing the way a newspaper would. Shravya was trained and tested on that kind of speech as well as on read sentences.

It writes Devanagari in two styles. Standard gives you the spelling of written Nepali. Verbatim keeps the words as they were said, which is what you want for a call or an interview.

Try it

Request access on the model page. We approve by hand. Then:

pip install onnxruntime numpy soundfile soxr huggingface_hub
hf auth login
hf download fiste-ai/shravya --local-dir shravya-model
python shravya-model/shravya.py clip.wav

In Python:

import sys
sys.path.insert(0, "shravya-model")
import shravya

model = shravya.load()
print(model.transcribe("clip.wav"))                      # standard spelling
print(model.transcribe("call.wav", style="verbatim"))    # the way it was said

It reads wav, flac, ogg and mp3 at any sample rate. Recordings longer than 40 seconds are cut at quiet points and transcribed piece by piece. A hosted demo will follow.

What it is

TaskNepali speech to Devanagari text
TypeConformer transducer, 119.6M parameters, fine-tuned on 174 hours of Nepali speech
OutputDevanagari without punctuation, in standard or verbatim spelling. English words are written in Devanagari
Speed28 times faster than real time on 8 threads of an Intel Core i7-10700K; about 1.2 GB of memory
NeedsPython with onnxruntime and numpy. No deep learning framework, no graphics card, no internet
ModeOffline, a whole utterance at a time. Not streaming yet
LicenceWeights CC BY-NC 4.0, by request. Runtime MIT

Training data, the checks we ran on the published files and the acknowledgements are on the model card.

Where it falls short

  • Calls are still hard. 38.2% word error rate on real calls is not good enough without a person checking.
  • No punctuation and no Latin letters. Numbers come out sometimes as digits and sometimes as words.
  • Names, numbers and addresses are the words it gets wrong most. Check a phone number or an amount before you use it.
  • Offline only. It needs the whole utterance. Streaming is next.
  • Not tested on children, strong regional accents, far-away microphones, music or loud noise.

Questions

What is Shravya?
Shravya is a speech recognition model for the Nepali language made by Fiste AI. You give it Nepali speech and it writes the words in Devanagari. It handles conversations, phone calls, podcasts and lectures with English words mixed in, as well as read speech.
How accurate is Shravya?
Over five test sets its mean word error rate is 23.5%, or 20.4% after spelling normalisation, the lowest of the 16 publicly available Nepali systems we have measured. On read sentences from FLEURS it is 23.9%. Shravya was trained on the same kinds of speech as the five test sets, which most other systems were not.
Does Shravya work on phone calls?
It was trained with a simulated telephone channel and some telephone speech, and it scores 38.2% word error rate on NepTel 0.2, a set of real calls. Rani by Ampixa Labs is ahead on that set at 33.5%. Neither number is good enough to transcribe calls without a person checking.
Does Shravya need a graphics card or the internet?
No. It runs offline on an ordinary processor, about 28 times faster than real time on a desktop machine, and needs only the onnxruntime and numpy Python packages.
Is Shravya free, and can I use it commercially?
The weights are free for research and other non-commercial use under CC BY-NC 4.0, by request on Hugging Face. The runtime code is MIT. For commercial use, open an issue on GitHub and tell us what you need.
What does the name Shravya mean?
श्रव्य (shravya) is the Nepali and Sanskrit word for audible, that which can be heard, as in श्रव्य-दृश्य, audio-visual.
